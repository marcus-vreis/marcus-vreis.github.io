Master's research in Computer Science at **UFSCar** (advised by Prof. Dr.
Jurandy Gomes de Almeida Junior). Investigates how object detectors and
teacher–student self-training behave when target imagery undergoes severe
domain shift — parametric corruptions (**COCO-C**), dense fog (**Foggy
Cityscapes**, $\beta \in \{0.005, 0.01, 0.02\}$) and day $\to$ night transition
(**BDD100K**).

The study grew out of a rigorously measured negative result: using crop-level
vision-language classifiers (**CLIP**, **SigLIP**) or dense visual features
(**DINOv2 k-NN**) as a semantic filter to clean a teacher detector's
pseudo-labels (Faster R-CNN / RetinaNet) **degrades AUPRC ranking across 100%
of tested conditions** (Finding A1). Headroom analysis exposed the two
structural causes: an isolated crop score loses the localisation context the
detector already used, and under severe shift the teacher's primary failure
mode is not false-positive inflation — it is **recall collapse** (Finding A3).
Where filtering is needed most, the ceiling for filtering is lowest.

The actionable finding lies in the opposite mechanism: **pseudo-label
completion** using a frozen open-vocabulary detector (**Grounding DINO**),
which remains complementary to the closed-set teacher under domain shift
(Finding A2). Recovering objects missed by the teacher rather than pruning its
predictions lifts pseudo-label recall by **+0.077** and F1 by **+0.023**,
driving teacher–student self-training on a single consumer GPU (RTX 5060 Ti
16 GB). The work also includes independent reproductions of recent methods
(**TTAOD-F**, NeurIPS 2025; **CCKM**, IEEE TIP 2026).
