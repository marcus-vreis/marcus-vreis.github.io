M.Sc. research in Computer Science at [PPGCC/UFSCar](https://www.ppgcc.dc.ufscar.br/), advised by [Prof. Dr. Jurandy Gomes de Almeida Junior](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=a4L1sbcAAAAJ). The study evaluates object detectors and teacher-student self-training pipelines when test images undergo severe domain shift, covering parametric synthetic corruptions (COCO-C), dense fog (Foggy Cityscapes, $\beta \in \{0.005, 0.01, 0.02\}$), and day-to-night transitions (BDD100K).

Initial experiments tested crop-level vision-language classifiers (CLIP and SigLIP) and dense visual features (DINOv2 k-NN) as semantic filters over pseudo-labels generated by a closed-set teacher detector (Faster R-CNN and RetinaNet). Crop filtering reduced AUPRC across 100% of evaluated conditions (Finding A1). Ceiling analysis isolated two causes for this drop. Scoring an isolated bounding-box crop discards the spatial context already used by the detector, and under heavy shift the primary failure mode of the teacher is recall collapse rather than false-positive inflation (Finding A3).

When recall collapses, pruning candidate boxes further starves student supervision. The alternative evaluated in this work is pseudo-label completion using a frozen open-vocabulary detector (Grounding DINO), whose predictions exhibit low error overlap with the teacher under domain shift (Finding A2). Merging complementary open-vocabulary detections recovers objects missed by the teacher, increasing pseudo-label recall by +0.077 and F1 by +0.023. The research codebase also maintains independent reproductions of TTAOD-F (NeurIPS 2025) and CCKM (IEEE TIP 2026).
